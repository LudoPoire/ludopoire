abstract: |
  Acinetobacter baumannii is a nosocomial pathogen whose capacity for natural transformation may contribute to the acquisition and spread of antibiotic resistance. We show that calcium is a potent inducer of natural transformation in A. baumannii. Submillimolar concentrations of calcium ions upregulate type IV pilus genes and transformation-specific genes such as comEA and dprA in a growth phase-dependent manner. Calcium also directly interacts with the calcium-dependent adhesin PilY1, supporting type IV pilus dynamics and DNA capture. These findings reveal that calcium regulates natural transformation through both transcriptional and structural mechanisms and provide a robust protocol for genetic engineering of A. baumannii by natural transformation.
